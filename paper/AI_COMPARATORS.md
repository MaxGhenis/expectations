# AI growth comparator audit

Verified 5 September 2026. Numerical comparisons must match geography, output concept, target period, forecast date and conditionality. This audit supersedes treating the previous literature compilation's heterogeneous 0.1–30 pp range as a single comparable forecast range.

## Karger et al. (2026)

[Official HTML](https://forecastingresearch.org/research/economic-effects-of-ai), [official PDF](https://forecastingresearch.org/pdf/economic-effects-of-ai.pdf), [NBER record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35046).

Publication: 31 March 2026, HTML revised 20 May 2026. Figure 5, PDF page 18, was inspected visually. Its AI-expert panel labels the fitted pooled probability above 10% as follows:

| Target growth window | Unconditional | Conditional on rapid AI |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2025–29 | 0.0% | 3.5% |
| 2045–49 | 1.4% | 10.4% |

The plot rounds these values. The outcomes are five-year annualized US GDP growth, not annual growth in 2030 or 2050. Distributions are fitted to elicited quantiles. The near-term unconditional entry does not support a large unconditional extreme-tail contrast with SPF. The longer-term entries lie beyond SPF density horizons. No multi-year matched comparison is presented in this revision.

## Other primary sources

- [Acemoglu, The Simple Macroeconomics of AI](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/2024-10/The%20Simple%20Macroeconomics%20of%20AI.pdf): task-based productivity effects over a decade. An incremental TFP effect is neither total GDP growth nor a forecast probability.
- [Davidson, Could Advanced AI Drive Explosive Economic Growth?](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/could-advanced-ai-drive-explosive-economic-growth/): global growth above 30% per year before 2100. Relevant to the range of possible growth regimes; not a US/euro-area annual forecast within SPF horizons.
- [Erdil and Besiroglu, Explosive growth from AI automation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11690): mechanisms and constraints associated with broad automation. Do not treat a scenario conditional on advanced AI as an unconditional probability at an SPF target date.

The appropriate current claim is that the SPF distributions quantify a professional baseline against which near-term, unconditional high-growth beliefs can be compared. The cited evidence does not establish a single commensurable probability difference between the SPF panel and a second population.
